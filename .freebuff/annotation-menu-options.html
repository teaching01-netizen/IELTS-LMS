The toolbar and iOS's own menu — three ways to stop sharing a lane

iOS paints its selection menu on top of everything and takes the lane above the selection whenever there is room. Our floating surface prefers that same lane on touch, which is what the screenshot shows: Copy / Find Selection / Look Up / Translate drawn over our swatches. Nothing can move the native menu — it is browser chrome, not a node in our DOM — so placement policy is the only lever. Same passage, same selected cell, same menu, same 424×660 frame in all three options below.

A. Take the opposite lane · recommended

On touch the toolbar prefers below the selection. iOS wants above, so we stop wanting it.

Floats, and cannot overlap the menu.

  • One rule, not two: below on touch, whichever lane iOS happens to take.
  • When below has no comfortable room → the sheet, at the bottom of the visible region.
  • Mouse: unchanged — above, exactly as now.

B. Keep the side, add distance

Keep preferring above, but reserve the menu's real height (~200px) and clear it.

A toolbar stranded at the top of the screen.
  • It clears the menu, but the tools now read as unrelated to the words that raised them.
  • Any selection lower on the page runs out of room above and docks — so on a phone B behaves like C most of the time.
  • It still collides when the selection is high enough that iOS flips its own menu below: that is the one case where "above" is not the menu's lane.
  • If the surface is ever taller (large accessibility text wraps the swatches onto three rows), the reserve no longer fits at all.

C. Always dock on touch

Never float on a coarse pointer: the sheet always sits at the bottom of the visible region.

“No soak”

Highlight

Zero collisions, always.
  • The menu keeps its lane; ours is a different part of the screen.
  • Cost: the sheet covers the question the student is reading, and the tools are never next to the words — they have to look away from what they selected.
  • It makes the sheet the touch default, which reverses the rule we agreed: float when there is comfortable room, dock only when there is not.

The decision, case by case

Situation A · opposite lane B · more distance C · always dock
Selected cell mid-passage on a phone, menu above Floats below ✓ (the frame above) Floats above, ~130px away Docked
Selection high on a phone — iOS has no room above and flips its menu below Docks (we will not fight for the lane it took) Collides — we were above, so did the menu Docked
iPad landscape, room on both sides Floats below ✓ Floats above, far Docked
Software keyboard up, visible region short Docks Docks Docked
Desktop / mouse (no native menu exists) Floats above — unchanged Floats above, far Floats above (if we exempt the fine pointer)

A is the only one that keeps the tools beside the words and leaves the menu's lane free in the common case; it pays for that by docking somewhat more often on phones, where the bottom half of a paragraph is often the only room that is genuinely conflict-free. Either way the numbers — the reserved lane, the menu's height, the comfort buffer — stay tokens in src/index.css, so this can be tuned on hardware without touching the engine.
